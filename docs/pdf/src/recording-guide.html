EchoCV Recording Guide

How to record your sessions for the EchoCV dataset

Your participant code: Coordinator: Shared folder:

Why we record

EchoCV is our AI interview coach. To work well on a real laptop webcam, it has to learn from people like its users. Your recordings teach it to:

  • spot behaviours such as touching your face, crossing your arms, slouching and reading notes;
  • hear Indonesian filler sounds like "eee", "hmm" and "em";
  • check eye contact, smiling and posture.

No public dataset shows these from a laptop webcam, so we record our own.

At a glance

  • 3 sessions per person, on different days, about 30 minutes each.
  • Each session has two parts:
    • Part A: 10 short clips of 30 seconds, acting out one behaviour per clip.
    • Part B: 4 interview answers of 1–2 minutes, answered naturally.
  • Rename every file right after you record it.
  • Upload only to the team's shared folder. Never through WhatsApp, email or anything public.

Before your first session

  • Sign the consent form (once).
  • Get your participant code from the coordinator (for example p01).
  • Read this guide once from start to finish.

1. Set up your laptop

Camera and microphone

  • Use your laptop's built-in webcam, with the laptop on a desk where you would normally use it. That is the camera EchoCV runs on.
  • Don't use a phone or a camera on a tripod. Use an external webcam only if that's what you really use for online interviews, and tell the coordinator.
  • Use the built-in microphone. Earphones or a headset mic are fine if that's what you normally use; tell the coordinator.

Turn off all camera effects

Effects that change your face or the picture ruin the recording. "Eye contact" correction is the worst: it makes you look at the camera when you aren't, which is exactly what EchoCV measures.

  • Windows Studio Effects (Settings › Bluetooth & devices › Cameras › your camera): turn off Eye contact, Background effects (blur), Automatic framing, Portrait light, Creative filters and Voice focus. Also check the Studio effects tile in Quick Settings.
  • Laptop brand camera apps (Lenovo, ASUS, HP, Dell and others): turn off auto-framing, eye correction, beautify and background effects.
  • NVIDIA Broadcast or AMD camera effects: turn them off or close the app.
  • Mac: Control Center › Video Effects: turn off Center Stage, Portrait, Studio Light and Reactions.
  • Close video-call apps and browser tabs that use the camera.

Framing, light and privacy

  • Sit 50–80 cm from the screen, with the screen tilted as you normally use it.
  • The picture shows your whole head down to mid-chest. Don't zoom and don't lean in.
  • Crossed arms and notes held at your chest should show at least partly at the bottom. If they don't, tilt the screen back a little once, then keep it there for the whole session.
  • Put a small sticker right next to the webcam lens and look at it whenever you're asked to look at the camera.
  • Your face must be clearly visible. A dim room or a window behind you is fine for one session at most.
  • Privacy: no other people in view or audible, since they haven't agreed to be recorded. Nothing readable that identifies anyone: no ID or student cards, letters, or chat windows behind you.

3. Before you press record (every session)

  • Laptop plugged in, on a desk, screen at its normal angle.
  • All camera effects off.
  • Sticker next to the lens; the picture shows head to mid-chest.
  • Quiet room, nobody else in view, phone on silent.
  • Props within reach: a sheet of paper with notes (print or copy the question list, nothing personal), your phone, and a book or notebook.
  • A 3-second test video plays back with sound. Delete it afterwards.

4. Part A: 10 behaviour clips

  1. Press record and sit still for 2 seconds.
  2. Do the behaviour for 30 seconds (watch the app's timer).
    • Keep talking the whole time: describe your day, explain a recipe, or count slowly. Your mouth moves in a real interview, so it should here too.
    • Change the variation every ~5 seconds: other hand, higher, lower, stronger, weaker. Thirty seconds of one frozen pose teaches EchoCV only one picture.
  3. Stop, then rename the file straight away (see section 6).

One behaviour per clip. The file name tells EchoCV what is in the clip, so a mixed clip teaches it the wrong thing. If you mix by mistake, delete the clip and record it again.

5. Part B: 4 interview answers

Be yourself here. Don't act out behaviours and don't hide them. If you normally say "eee", touch your chin or cross your arms, that's fine. These answers show how EchoCV does in a real interview.

For each answer:

  1. Read the question on your screen. Take a few seconds to think if you like.
  2. Press record and look at the lens for 2 seconds.
  3. Answer in 1–2 minutes, talking to the lens as if the interviewer were behind it. No script and no reading. "Eee", "hmm" and pauses are fine; they're exactly what we need.
  4. Stop and rename the file.
  • Language: answer most questions in Indonesian. If you're comfortable, answer one per session in English. Mixing Indonesian and English naturally is fine.
  • Keep it private: no real names (yours or anyone else's), addresses, phone numbers, NIK, student numbers or company secrets. Say "teman saya" or "dosen saya" instead of names. Making up details is fine; it's practice.
  • Which questions: session 1 answers q01–q04, session 2 answers q05–q08, session 3 answers q09–q12.

8. Check before you upload

Re-record instead of trimming or editing. Editing apps re-encode the video and can shift the sound.

  • Every file plays to the end with sound.
  • Part A clips are at least 32 seconds (2 seconds still + 30 seconds). Answers are 1–2 minutes.
  • Your whole face is in the picture the whole time (brief turns are fine).
  • File names follow the pattern exactly; 10 clips and 4 answers in the session folder.
  • Nobody else is visible or audible, and nothing identifying is readable.
  • Camera effects were off (the background is sharp, not blurred).
  • Each Part A clip shows only its own behaviour.

9. Upload

  • Upload the whole session folder to the team's shared folder (link from the coordinator).
  • Never send recordings through WhatsApp, Telegram or other chat apps (they shrink the video and leave copies on phones), by email, to GitHub, or through any public link or social media.
  • Once the coordinator confirms your upload arrived, delete the videos from your laptop, including Camera Roll and the Recycle Bin.

10. Your privacy

  • Your videos are labelled with your code, not your name.
  • They are kept in a restricted folder that only the team can open, and they are never published.
  • You can stop any recording, or ask for any clip to be deleted.
  • You can withdraw at any time, without giving a reason, by telling the coordinator. Your recordings are then deleted within 30 days.
  • The full terms are in the consent form you signed.
